# Methodological Note: Interpretation and Expertise

No one really knows what a poem is written about apart from the poet themselves. And even then, a poem often exceeds its author's intentions. Nobody knows your lived experiences as a reader and everything that led up to you reading a poem at a particular moment in time.

This study does not claim there is a correct interpretation of any poem. There are different interpretations, shaped by experience, knowledge, lived experience, and in some cases domain expertise.

What we are comparing is not "right versus wrong" readings. We are comparing what different kinds of readers bring to a text: what they notice, what they miss, what they are confident about, and where that confidence is misplaced.

Claude's readings are one interpretation. Sam's readings are another, informed by the fact that he is a published poet, that one of the poems is his own, and that he has spent a career thinking about language, communication, and the gap between what is said and what is understood.

The finding is not that Claude reads poems badly. The finding is about what is systematically present and systematically absent in Claude's readings, and what that tells us about the difference between pattern recognition and comprehension.

When we say Claude "misses" something, we mean: a reader with relevant experience, knowledge, or embodied understanding would likely read this differently. Not that Claude is wrong. That it is incomplete in ways that are consistent and revealing.

A tension should be acknowledged: the coding practice inevitably frames Sam's readings as the reference point against which Claude's are measured. This is inherent to a single-coder study. A second coder would introduce an independent perspective and strengthen the analysis. The coded responses should be read as one expert reader's assessment of where the readings diverge, not as definitive judgements of quality.
