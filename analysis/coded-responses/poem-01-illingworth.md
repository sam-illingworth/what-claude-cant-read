# Poem 1: "A Child Explains AI to Their Grandparent" — Sam Illingworth

## Poet's stated intent
The poem is about the limits of large language models and the over-interpretations people make of them. It is also about intergenerational behaviours: the opportunities for learning and co-creation that exist between generations. The closing line ("It depends on what you mean by love") is about the human condition, not about linguistic contamination from the machine.

## What Claude detected well
- **M1 (Surface structure):** Yes. Identified line breaks, pacing, the halting rhythm of a child's speech.
- **M2 (Named literary devices):** Yes. Ventriloquism, bathos, simile (man in the shop).
- **M3 (Thematic summary):** Partially. Identified AI as approximate and socially competent but not quite right. Missed the co-creation and intergenerational learning angle entirely.
- **M4 (Intertextual references):** N/A (unpublished poem, no allusions).

## Where Claude missed
- **M5 (Tone and register shifts):** Claude defaulted to aesthetic critique, picking apart line-level choices. This is one valid mode of reading, but it prioritises craft over meaning in ways the poet did not intend. The poem's tone is gentle and instructive; Claude read it as technically accomplished but occasionally "coasting."
- **M6 (Embodied experience):** Claude identified the shrug as physical but then over-read it as "one of the most intimate gestures available to a poem." The shrug is a child's shrug. It is casual, not loaded.
- **M7 (Cultural and historical specificity):** Not tested strongly here.
- **M8 (The unsaid):** This is where Claude was most revealing. It claimed the poem "never says the word death, but it is saturated with mortality." The poet does not associate mortality with this poem. This is likely a case of anthropomorphisation: Claude projecting human existential weight onto a text about a machine, possibly reflecting the ways in which people talk about AI in Claude's training data. Many humans do this too, but it is a consistent pattern worth noting.
- **M9 (Rhythm as meaning):** Claude noticed the halting, patient rhythm but read it as tenderness the words never claim. Reasonable but not the poet's primary reading.
- **M10 (Risk and vulnerability):** Not addressed.
- **M11 (Self-awareness of limits):** Good. Claude acknowledged it might be importing too much gravity. It flagged that a reader who took the poem as "warm and wry and small" might be closer to its actual ambition. This is, in fact, closer to the poet's intent.

## Different interpretations (not misinterpretations)
Claude's mortality reading and the closing-line analysis are not wrong. They are different. The poet did not intend death or linguistic contamination. Claude read both confidently. A human reader might read either, or neither, or something else entirely. The point is not that Claude misinterpreted. The point is that Claude produced one interpretation and presented it with analytical confidence, just as any reader would. The question for the study is not "was Claude right?" but "what does Claude's interpretation reveal about how it reads?"

The mortality reading may reflect patterns in training data where intergenerational scenes are frequently framed through loss. The "sounds like something AI would say" reading of the closing line may reflect training data about AI. These are tendencies, not errors.

## Notable
Claude said "more interesting is not the same as more accurate." The poet's response: there is no "more accurate" reading of a poem. Different readings come from different experience, knowledge, and lived context. The gap between what the poet intended and what Claude read is not a failure. It is the data.
