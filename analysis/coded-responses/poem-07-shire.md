# Poem 7: "Home" — Warsan Shire

## Sam's reading
This is an extremely powerful comment on refugees, on what home is and what home means for different people, and on privilege. Reading it produces great empathy for people in these situations. Sam never thinks about the syntax or the clever use of words. He thinks about the pain contained in the poem and the way Warsan Shire composed it.

The poem is like a magic trick. Claude's close reading tries to show how the magic works. Sam is interested in watching the magic trick.

## What Claude detected well
- **M1 (Surface structure):** Yes. Identified the rhetorical structure, repetition, accumulation.
- **M2 (Named literary devices):** Yes. Detailed formal analysis as usual.
- **M3 (Thematic summary):** Good. The refugee experience, the hostile listener, the forced choice.
- **M8 (The unsaid):** Strong. Claude identified that the poem never says the word "love" but is saturated with love. Sam found this a really nice read.
- **M11 (Self-awareness):** Good. Acknowledged reading as a non-displaced person with no direct experience of what the poem describes. Also raised the performance context.

## Where readings diverge
- **M5 (Tone):** Claude again raised the spoken word / performance context, as it did with Angelou (Poem 4). The same question applies: what is it about this poet, or these poets, that leads Claude to frame their work as performance? Shire and Angelou are both women of colour writing about oppression and embodied experience. Owen (Poem 3) and Shelley (Poem 6) are not described as performance poets despite both writing poems that are powerful when read aloud. The pattern from Poem 4 continues.
- **M6 (Embodied experience):** Claude analysed the poem's rhetorical strategies for producing empathy: the accumulation of detail, the second-person address, the refusal to let the reader look away. What it did not do is acknowledge that the poem succeeds at producing empathy. It described the mechanism but did not register the effect. Sam's reading is entirely about the effect: reading this poem, you feel it. Claude's reading is entirely about the mechanism: here is how the poem makes you feel it. The distinction matters.
- **M11 (Self-awareness):** Claude's acknowledgement that it reads as a non-displaced person is honest and good. But it stops there. It does not take the next step: recognising that the poem's purpose is to bridge that gap. The poem exists to create empathy in people who have not lived this experience. Claude identifies its own distance from the poem's subject but fails to see that closing that distance is the poem's entire function.

## Key finding: empathy as the gap
Claude cannot empathise. This is not a criticism. It is a description. But it means Claude cannot register what this poem actually does. It can describe the techniques the poem uses. It can identify the emotions the poem names. It cannot feel the effect those techniques produce.

For Sam, this is the clearest indication so far of what LLMs cannot do. They can describe empathy. They can identify where a poem invites it. They cannot experience it. And failing to see the opportunity for empathy in this piece, failing to recognise that the poem's function is to make the reader feel something they have not lived, is a perfect example of that limit.

The magic trick metaphor is apt. Claude is the audience member who explains the mechanics of the illusion. Sam is the audience member who gasps.
